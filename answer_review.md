## 5. [in_scope] What is the difference between theft and robbery?

> Theft – Under [BNS §303], theft is the dishonest taking of any movable property out of a person’s possession without that person’s consent. The offence is complete when the offender moves the property with the intention of dishonestly depriving the owner of it [§303].
> 
> Robbery – [BNS §309] makes robbery a *theft (or extortion) that is accompanied by violence or the threat of instant death, hurt or wrongful restraint*.  
> * If, in committing the theft, the offender voluntarily causes or attempts to cause death, hurt, wrongful restraint, or the fear of any of those, the theft becomes robbery [§309(2)].  
> * Extortion also becomes robbery when the offender, in the presence of the victim, puts the victim (or another person) in fear of instant death, hurt or restraint to obtain the property [§309(3)].
> 
> Key difference  
> - Theft requires only a dishonest taking of property.  
> - Robbery adds the element of *force or intimidation* (death, hurt, or restraint) used at the time of the taking (or to obtain the property), turning the simple taking into a violent offence.  
> 
> Thus, robbery is essentially theft plus the use or threat of immediate physical injury or restraint.

> Right of private defence under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita
> 
> 1. Who may claim it – Every person has a right, subject to the restrictions in § 37, to defend  
>    * his own body and the body of any other person against any offence affecting the human body; and  
>    * his own or any other person’s movable or immovable property against an offence of theft, robbery, mischief or criminal trespass (or an attempt thereof). [BNS §35]
> 
> 2. When it begins  
>    * Body – The right commences as soon as a reasonable apprehension of danger to the body arises from an attempt or threat to commit the offence and continues while that apprehension persists. [BNS §40]  
>    * Property – The right commences when a reasonable apprehension of danger to the property arises. [BNS §43(a)]
> 
> 3. How long it continues –  
>    * Against theft, until the offender retreats with the property, the public authority intervenes, or the property is recovered. [BNS §43(b)]  
>    * Against robbery, as long as the offender is causing or attempting to cause death, hurt or wrongful restraint, or while the fear of instant death/hurt persists. [BNS §43(c)]  
>    * Against criminal trespass or mischief, while the offender continues the commission of the act. [BNS §43(d)]  
>    * Against house‑breaking after sunset and before sunrise, while the house‑trespass begun by the breaking continues. [BNS §43(e)]
> 
> 4. Extent of force – The defender may use only that force which is necessary; he may not inflict more harm than is necessary for the purpose of defence. [BNS §37(2)]
> 
> 5. When death may be caused – If the offence that triggers the defence is robbery, house‑breaking after sunset, mischief by fire or explosive on a dwelling, or theft/mischief/criminal trespass where death or grievous hurt is reasonably feared, the defender may lawfully cause death or other harm to the wrong‑doer. [BNS §41]
> 
> 6. Risk to innocent persons – If the defender cannot exercise the right without risking harm to an innocent person, his right extends to taking that risk. [BNS §44]
> 
> 7. Acts excluded from the right – The right does not apply against an act that does not reasonably cause apprehension of death or grievous hurt when the act (or its direction) is done by a public servant acting in good faith, or when the defender can obtain protection from public authorities. [BNS §37(1)]
> 
> 8. Legal consequence – Anything done in the lawful exercise of the right of private defence is not an offence. [BNS §34]

> Under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita a police officer (or any person) may lawfully make an arrest without a warrant when the law itself obliges him to do so.
